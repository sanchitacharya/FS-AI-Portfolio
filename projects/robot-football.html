01 · Context

What is STRIDE?

STRIDE is a university robot football team working with ROBOTIS OP3 humanoid robots. To play, a robot has to answer two questions all the time: where is everything else on the pitch, and where am I?

My work covers both. Each piece is built and tested in simulation first, then taken to the real robot and its camera.

02 · Project

Phase 1 · Simulation + real robot

Multi-object tracking

This is the first phase of STRIDE’s visual localisation project: a ground-truth tracking system. Static cameras around the field track the robots and the ball in field coordinates. These positions are used only during development, to train and test the robot’s own localisation.

  • Done: built the tracker. Every robot and the ball keeps a stable ID, with position and velocity in field coordinates.
  • Done: a very simple simulation version for testing: one robot and one ball. The simulated camera doesn’t give 3D positions like the OAK-D, so positions come from the Webots supervisor.
  • Done: real version built on the OAK-D camera, which gives 3D positions of robots and the ball. The field is anchored on an X-shaped line intersection.
  • Next: record the real version on the field. It should work; it just hasn’t been filmed yet.
  • Next: track multiple robots at once on the real field.
  • Next: use it as ground truth to train and test the robot’s own localisation (Phase 2).

03 · Project

Phase 2 · First version in simulation

Odometry-based localization with visual landmarks

This is the second phase of STRIDE’s visual localisation project: the robot works out its own position on the field using its onboard camera and motion.

  • Done: the robot localises itself whenever it sees an anchor point (a goalpost at a known position), using the IMU for heading.
  • Done: the ball is placed on the same field map. When the camera loses sight of it, it is dimmed and its last-seen position is kept.
  • Done: goalposts are the only anchor point for now, which is enough in simulation.
  • Next: use visual SLAM to keep track of position when no anchor point is in view.
  • Next: add more anchor points on the real field.
  • Next: detect and track other robots, and mark them as teammate or opponent.

04 · Tools

Stack (high level)

Both projects are ROS 2 nodes in C++. Simulation is used to iterate quickly, and the real OP3 robots with an OAK-D camera are the final test.

  • C++ · Eigen

  • Data association
